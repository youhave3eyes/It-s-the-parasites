Six rules that make or break a cleanse

Most bad cleanse experiences come from doing the steps out of order. Follow these and the rest gets easy.

1. Poop before you kill

Aim for 1 to 3 bowel movements a day before you start any killing herbs. If waste can't leave, what you kill stays inside and makes you feel sick.

2. Shield, then sword

Break the biofilm first in the morning, then take the kill herbs. Herbs work better on targets that can't hide.

3. Bind two hours apart

Binders soak up everything nearby, including your herbs, vitamins and medications. Take them 2 hours away from all of it.

4. Go in rounds

Herbs hit adults better than eggs. Running the kill phase in rounds, with a rest week in between, catches the ones that hatch later.

5. Starve them while you fight

Cut sugar, white flour, alcohol and processed food for the whole protocol. Eat fruit, greens, seeds, cooked grains and lots of water.

6. Go slow if it hits hard

Headache, tiredness, bloating or a rash can mean you're clearing faster than you can drain. Lower the herbs, raise the binder and water, and rest.

Don't do this protocol without a doctor if

You're pregnant or breastfeeding, it's for a child, you have liver, kidney or seizure conditions, you take blood thinners or prescription medications, or you have surgery coming up. If tests confirm a parasite, prescription antiparasitics are fast and effective and can be used alongside this approach.

02
Phase 02 · Every morning of a round

Biofilm: break the shield

A biofilm is a slimy shield that microbes build on the gut wall out of sugars, proteins and minerals. Bacteria and yeast hide under it, protected from your immune system and from anything you take.

Think of plaque on your teeth. That's a biofilm. Your gut can have its own.

Evidence

Biofilms are well proven for bacteria and yeast like candida. The idea that parasites hide in or behind gut biofilms comes from holistic practice and hasn't been well studied. It's still the logic behind doing this step first.

Phase 03 · With meals during a round

Kill: clear what's hiding

The classic holistic combination is black walnut hull, wormwood and clove. Each one is said to target a different stage of the parasite's life. The others on this list are add-ons or swaps.

Adults

Black walnut hull

The green hull, high in juglone and tannins. Traditionally used against adult worms.

Larvae

Wormwood

One of the oldest worm herbs, named for it. Its compounds are the base of real antimalarial drugs.

Eggs

Clove

Rich in eugenol. Holistic protocols use it to target eggs so the cycle doesn't restart.

Die-off ("herx")

Headache, tiredness, bloating, achy joints, a rash or bad mood in the first days. Lower the kill herbs, raise water and binders, and make sure you're going to the bathroom.

Stop and get medical help for

Fever, severe belly pain, blood in your stool, yellow skin or eyes, confusion, a seizure, or vomiting you can't keep water down through.

Phase 04 · Twice a day, 2 hours apart from everything

Bind: carry it out

When parasites and microbes die, they release waste and toxins. Binders grab that waste in your gut so it leaves in your stool instead of getting reabsorbed.

Binders are the garbage bags. Without them, the trash spills back in.

Simple binder stack

Charcoal or clay in the mid-morning slot, chlorella or psyllium in the afternoon. Rotating binders spreads out what they grab and keeps you from getting backed up.

The 2-hour rule

Binders don't pick and choose. Taken too close, they soak up your herbs, vitamins and medications, including birth control. Keep 2 hours on both sides.
